This paper investigates the 'Granularity Paradox' in time-series forecasting, showing that finer temporal disaggregation (e.g., moving from Monthly to Weekly or Daily frequency) increases dataset size (N) and improves in-sample diagnostics, but paradoxically degrades out-of-sample forecast accuracy because errors compound recursively over the longer effective horizon (H) needed to reach a fixed calendar target. Conversely, coarser aggregation (e.g., Annual) removes recursive multi-step error propagation entirely but sacrifices the sample size needed for reliable parameter estimation, creating a fundamental bias-variance-horizon tradeoff that practitioners must navigate when choosing the temporal resolution of forecasting models.
