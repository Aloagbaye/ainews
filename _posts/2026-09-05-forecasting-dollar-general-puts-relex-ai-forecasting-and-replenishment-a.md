## Recent publications

#### RATL: Learning from Retrieved Residuals for Robust Multivariate Time-Series Forecasting

**Yuchen He, Yueyang Cang, Zhiyuan Ning, Ningyu Wang, Li Shi** · *arXiv preprint, 2026* · `arXiv:2609.03937`

RATL proposes retrieval-augmented residual learning for robust multivariate time-series forecasting. Instead of retrieving historical target trajectories, it retrieves past forecast residuals from a frozen base model and uses a learned router to combine them, making it relevant for practitioners building residual-correction or ensemble forecasting systems.

[Read paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.03937)

#### Compositional Spectral Prompts for LLM-based Online Time Series Forecasting

**Seungyoon Choi, Hyunchul Kim, Jae-Gil Lee, Chanyoung Park** · *CIKM 2026 / arXiv preprint, 2026* · `arXiv:2609.02093`

This paper introduces CoSPOT, an online time-series forecasting framework that uses frozen LLMs with compositional spectral prompts. Its focus on frequency-domain prompting and adaptation under distribution shift is useful for practitioners exploring LLM-based forecasting with limited fine-tuning overhead.

[Read paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.02093)

#### DynG-Diff: A State-Aware Dynamic Guidance Diffusion Framework for Probabilistic Time Series Forecasting

**Zhente Zhang, Zhengwei Ni, Wei Fan** · *arXiv preprint, 2026* · `arXiv:2609.02068`

DynG-Diff is a probabilistic time-series forecasting method built around diffusion models with dynamic, state-aware guidance. It targets robust multivariate forecasts under noisy or unevenly reliable observations, making it relevant for practitioners who need calibrated forecast distributions rather than only point forecasts.

[Read paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.02068)

#### Learning to Difference: Adaptive Reversible Differencing (AdaRDiff) for Time Series Forecasting

**Morad Laglil, Younes Hlal, Marouane El Hadari, Emilie Devijver, Eric Gaussier** · *arXiv preprint, 2026* · `arXiv:2608.28134`

AdaRDiff proposes an adaptive reversible differencing module for neural time-series forecasting. The method learns how to difference and reconstruct series in a plug-and-play way, which is practically relevant for improving forecasting backbones on data with trend, seasonality, or non-stationarity.

[Read paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.28134)

#### CEDAR: Controlled and Event-Driven Demand Forecasting via Residual Decomposition

**Junjie Meng, Ranxu Zhang, Zi-an Zhang, Shujun Liu, Xiaoning Qi, Xiaozhou Xu, Yanyong Zhang, Hui Xiong, Chao Wang** · *Proceedings of the 32nd ACM SIGKDD Conference on Knowledge Discovery and Data Mining (KDD 2026), ACM, 2026* · `arXiv:2608.25871; DOI: 10.1145/3770855.3818338`

CEDAR addresses controlled and event-driven demand forecasting by decomposing residuals and incorporating decisions and events into sales simulation. It is especially relevant for commercial forecasting teams because it targets e-commerce demand planning and is evaluated on large-scale Alibaba 1688 production data.
